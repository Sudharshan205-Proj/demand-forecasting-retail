# Phase 15 — Visualization Methodology

## 1. Visualization Objective

The purpose of visualization is to translate analytical outputs into
understandable evidence for stakeholders.

The visualization workflow therefore emphasizes:

- comparison;
- trend;
- variability;
- scenario analysis;
- forecasting evidence.

## 2. Demand Comparison

Average daily demand is shown by store to identify differences in observed
physical demand levels.

## 3. Variability

The coefficient of variation is used to compare relative demand variability.

CV = standard deviation / mean

A higher value indicates greater variability relative to average demand.

## 4. Forecast Evidence

The project communicates validated model evidence rather than presenting
unverified future forecasts as facts.

Phase 12 model evaluation remains the source of model-selection evidence.

## 5. Inventory Scenarios

Reorder-point scenarios are visualized across assumed lead times.

These are planning scenarios, not operational requirements.

## 6. Tableau

Tableau is used to provide interactive exploration of the established
analytical outputs.

The dashboard is designed for stakeholder interpretation rather than
technical model development.

## 7. Audience

The primary audience is assumed to be an internship-level business or
operations stakeholder who needs to understand:

- where demand is concentrated;
- which stores exhibit greater variability;
- how forecast evidence compares;
- how inventory scenarios change under assumptions.

## 8. Interpretation

Visualizations describe observed data and established analytical results.

They do not establish causality.

They do not imply that scenario assumptions represent actual supplier or
inventory policies.
