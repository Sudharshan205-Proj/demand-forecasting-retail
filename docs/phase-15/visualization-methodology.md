# Phase 15 — Visualization Methodology

## 1. Visualization Objective

The purpose of visualization is to translate analytical outputs into
understandable evidence for stakeholders.

The visualization workflow therefore emphasizes:

- comparison;
- trend;
- variability;
- scenario analysis;
- forecasting evidence.

## 2. Demand Comparison

Average daily demand is shown by store to identify differences in observed
physical demand levels.

The chart is a horizontal bar chart sorted ascending, with each bar labelled
with its value. The axis states that the measure is demand units over the
training period, so a reader cannot mistake it for a rate or a forecast. The
training-day count is carried in the underlying table because Store 4 has 60
training days against 532 for the others.

## 3. Variability

The coefficient of variation is used to compare relative demand variability.

CV = standard deviation / mean

A higher value indicates greater variability relative to average demand. The
workflow does not simply trust the stored column: it recomputes std / mean and
reconciles the result, and it checks that the stored variance equals the
squared standard deviation. Both checks are recorded in the quality report.

## 4. Forecast Evidence

The project communicates validated model evidence rather than presenting
unverified future forecasts as facts.

Phase 12 model evaluation remains the source of model-selection evidence. The
forecast-evidence chart plots the *lowest validation RMSE* per store and its
axis states explicitly that RMSE is measured in demand units and is not
comparable across stores of different size. The charted value is reconciled
against `tuned_validation_results.csv`; if the insight row is missing, the
workflow fails rather than drawing three of four figures.

## 5. Inventory Scenarios

Reorder-point scenarios are visualized across assumed lead times at a 95 %
service level.

These are planning scenarios, not operational requirements. The axis label
carries that caveat verbatim so it travels with the image.

The workflow verifies the scenario arithmetic rather than only plotting it:
expected lead-time demand = mean × lead time; safety stock = z × std ×
√lead time; reorder point = expected demand + safety stock; z equals the
standard-normal quantile of the service level; reorder points rise
monotonically with both service level and lead time; and every store covers
the full 3 × 3 grid.

## 6. Tableau

Tableau is used to provide interactive exploration of the established
analytical outputs.

The dashboard is designed for stakeholder interpretation rather than
technical model development. The workbook's cached textscan schema is
reconciled against the current CSV headers before it is used, because a stale
field order would mis-map columns.

## 7. Audience

The primary audience is an internship-level business or operations
stakeholder who needs to understand:

- where demand is concentrated;
- which stores exhibit greater variability;
- how forecast evidence compares;
- how inventory scenarios change under assumptions.

## 8. Interpretation

Visualizations describe observed data and established analytical results.

They do not establish causality.

They do not imply that scenario assumptions represent actual supplier or
inventory policies.

## 9. Reproducibility

Each figure is generated by code, and every run records the figure's byte size
and SHA-256 digest in `visualization_manifest.csv`. The workflow writes no
figure unless every input check has passed, so an image on disk always
corresponds to validated inputs.
