# Business Problem

## Project

Demand Forecasting for Retail

## Phase

Phase 1 — Business Understanding & Planning

## 1. Business Context

Retail businesses must continuously balance product availability against the cost and operational consequences of holding too much inventory.

If inventory is too low, customers may encounter stockouts and the business may lose potential sales.

If inventory is too high, the business may incur unnecessary holding costs, increase the risk of obsolete or slow-moving stock, and tie up working capital.

Demand forecasting can support inventory planning by estimating future product demand from historical observations and relevant demand drivers.

## 2. Problem Statement

The project addresses the following business problem:

> How can historical retail demand data be used to forecast future product demand so that inventory planning can be better informed?

The analysis will investigate historical sales patterns and, where the selected dataset supports them, the effects of promotions and holidays.

## 3. Primary Business Objective

The primary objective is to develop a reproducible demand forecasting workflow that estimates future product demand and translates those forecasts into useful inventory-planning insights.

## 4. Secondary Objectives

The project will:

1. Understand historical demand patterns.
2. Identify trends and seasonality.
3. Investigate relationships between demand and promotions.
4. Investigate relationships between demand and holidays.
5. Compare forecasting approaches.
6. Evaluate forecasting accuracy.
7. Identify products or periods where forecasting is more difficult.
8. Communicate findings through visualizations and dashboards.
9. Provide a usable forecasting application.
10. Document limitations and assumptions.

## 5. Why This Problem Matters

Accurate demand estimates can support:

- Inventory planning
- Replenishment planning
- Product prioritization
- Promotion planning
- Capacity planning
- Identification of high-demand periods
- Identification of potentially difficult-to-forecast products

The project will not claim that forecasts automatically optimize inventory. Forecasts are decision-support inputs.

## 6. Analytical Problem Type

The primary course-defined analytical problem type is:

### Making Predictions

The central task is predicting future retail demand from historical observations.

Secondary analytical problem types will also be investigated where supported by the data:

- Finding patterns
- Discovering connections
- Spotting something unusual
- Categorizing

The project will not artificially force all six analytical problem types into the final analysis.

## 7. Analytical Lifecycle

The project follows:

Ask → Prepare → Process → Analyze → Share → Act

Phase 1 corresponds primarily to:

### Ask

The project defines:

- The business problem

## 8. Expected Outcome

The expected outcome is a documented forecasting system capable of producing demand forecasts and communicating their implications for inventory planning.

Specific forecast values, model performance and business findings will only be documented after the relevant analyses are actually performed.

## 9. Important Boundary

No dataset-specific claims are made in this document.

Dataset dimensions, date ranges, product counts, store counts and other factual characteristics will be established during Phase 2.
