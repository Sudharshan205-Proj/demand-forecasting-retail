# Business Problem

## Project

Demand Forecasting for Retail

## Phase

Phase 1 — Business Understanding & Planning

## 1. Business context

Retail businesses must continuously balance product availability against the
cost and operational consequences of holding too much inventory.

If inventory is too low, customers may encounter stockouts and the business may
lose potential sales.

If inventory is too high, the business may incur unnecessary holding costs,
increase the risk of obsolete or slow-moving stock, and tie up working capital.

Demand forecasting supports inventory planning by estimating future product
demand from historical observations and relevant demand drivers.

## 2. Problem statement

The project addresses the following business problem:

> How can historical retail demand data be used to forecast future product
> demand so that inventory planning can be better informed?

The analysis investigates historical sales patterns and, where the dataset
supports them, the effects of promotions. The dataset has no holiday field, so
holiday effects are recorded as not applicable.

## 3. Primary business objective

The primary objective is a reproducible demand forecasting workflow that
estimates future product demand and translates those forecasts into useful
inventory-planning insights.

## 4. Secondary objectives

The project:

1. Characterizes historical demand patterns.
2. Identifies trends and seasonality.
3. Investigates relationships between demand and promotions.
4. Records the holiday question as not applicable for lack of data.
5. Compares forecasting approaches.
6. Evaluates forecasting accuracy.
7. Identifies where forecasting is more difficult.
8. Communicates findings through visualizations and dashboards.
9. Provides a usable forecasting application.
10. Documents limitations and assumptions.

## 5. Why this problem matters

Accurate demand estimates support:

- Inventory planning
- Replenishment planning
- Product prioritization
- Promotion planning
- Capacity planning
- Identification of high-demand periods
- Identification of difficult-to-forecast products

The project does not claim that forecasts automatically optimize inventory.
Forecasts are decision-support inputs.

## 6. Analytical problem type

The primary course-defined analytical problem type is:

### Making predictions

The central task is predicting future retail demand from historical
observations.

Secondary analytical problem types are investigated where the data supports
them:

- Finding patterns
- Discovering connections
- Spotting something unusual
- Categorizing

## 7. Analytical lifecycle

The project follows:

Ask → Prepare → Process → Analyze → Share → Act

Phase 1 corresponds primarily to:

### Ask

Phase 1 defines the business problem, context, stakeholders, objectives,
analytical questions, hypotheses and success criteria.

## 8. Expected outcome

The outcome is a documented forecasting system that produces demand forecasts
and communicates their implications for inventory planning.

Specific forecast values, model performance and business findings are documented
in the phase results documents where the analyses are performed.
